# The AI budtender is here. What should a dispensary ask before buying one?

If a customer asks for something the dispensary sold out of ten minutes ago, what does the AI say?

That’s where I’d start a vendor demo. A polished answer to a question about a strain tells you very little about what happens on a busy Friday when the menu changes and the person answering the phone has a line at the counter.

[Dutchie](https://dutchie.com/business/consumer-ai) describes a retail assistant connected to a store’s point of sale, menu, prices, and loyalty data. [BLAZE](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/blaze-unveils-herbie-ai-budtender-to-hyper-personalize-cannabis-e-commerce-302732271.html) introduced Herbie as a concierge for shoppers browsing an online dispensary. Those are the companies’ descriptions. Here’s what I’d ask each vendor to show me.

## Sell the last unit

Pick an item in a demo store. Sell the last unit at the register. Ask the assistant whether it’s available, then time how long its answer takes to catch up. Ask the same question from a second store location. A retailer needs to know which stock record the customer is hearing about and how old that record can be.

## Ask for a person

Give the assistant an ordinary problem that needs staff judgment. A customer says the price on their receipt differs from the price they saw online. Ask to speak to someone. Watch where the request goes, what the employee receives, and whether the customer has to explain everything again. Then try it after closing time. The handoff is part of the product you’re buying.

## Count the corrections

Before a pilot, choose one customer task and record how staff handle it today. During the pilot, count how often customers complete that task, how often staff have to correct an answer, and how much time those corrections take. Ask to inspect a few anonymized failures together. An average can hide the one recurring error your team spends every afternoon repairing.

I’d bring one of those questions to the next demo and ask the vendor to use a store employee’s example. The interesting part starts when somebody goes off script.
